title: Spot the scam
description: "Twelve printable message cards, some scams and some real, with an answer key naming each pressure and how to check."

**Time:** 10 minutes at tables, then 5 minutes together. **Setup:** print the cards below, one set per table, and cut them apart. Swap two or three for real messages your group has received, with every name, number, and link removed. Keep the answer key for yourself.

**Instructions for the group:** sort the cards into three piles: *scam*, *real*, and *can't tell yet*. For each scam, name the pressure. For each *can't tell yet*, say how you'd check safely.

All links and numbers here are fake.

## The cards

**Card 1, text message.**
> USPS: Your package is on hold due to an incomplete address. Update within 12 hours to avoid return: usps-redelivery-help.example.com

**Card 2, email.**
> Your monthly statement is ready. Sign in to the app or at the address on the back of your card to view it. We'll never ask for your password or a code by email.

**Card 3, phone call (read aloud).**
> "Hi, this is Dana from your bank's fraud department. We've blocked a $900 charge at an electronics store. To confirm you're the account holder, I'm sending a code to your phone. Can you read it back to me?"

**Card 4, text message.**
> Hi Mom, I dropped my phone in the toilet lol. This is my new number. Can you text me back here? Need a favor

**Card 5, pop-up on a computer screen.**
> WARNING! Your computer is infected with 5 viruses. Do not close this window. Call Microsoft Support immediately at 1-555-0148.

**Card 6, email to a bookkeeper.**
> From: accounts@supplier-co.example.com
> Hi, please note our bank has changed. Kindly use the attached details for the outstanding invoice and all future payments. We need this settled today to avoid a hold on your orders.

**Card 7, text message.**
> Your verification code is 482913. Don't share this code with anyone. We'll never call to ask for it.

**Card 8, social media message.**
> Hey! You won't believe it, I made $14,000 last month with this crypto platform. My mentor can show you too. It's limited spots, message me today!

**Card 9, email.**
> Your password was changed on Tuesday at 10:42 PM. If this was you, you can ignore this email. If not, secure your account from the app or by visiting the website directly.

**Card 10, phone call (read aloud).**
> "This is Officer Reyes with the county sheriff's office. You missed jury duty and there's a warrant for your arrest. You can resolve it today with a payment in gift cards. Don't hang up, or we'll have to send a deputy."

**Card 11, email at work.**
> From: the CEO (personal email address)
> Are you at your desk? I need you to pick up some gift cards for a client today. Keep this quiet, it's a surprise. I'll reimburse you.

**Card 12, text message.**
> Reminder: your dentist appointment is tomorrow at 2:30 PM. Reply C to confirm or call the office to reschedule.

## Answer key

| Card | Answer | Pressures | How to check |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Scam | Hurry | USPS texts only people who asked for tracking updates, and its texts don't contain links. Use the tracking number from the seller, on the official site. |
| 2 | Real (probably) | None | It sends you to the app or the card, not a link. Still, sign in your usual way, not from the email. |
| 3 | Scam | Fear, hurry | Anyone who asks for a code you received is trying to sign in as you. Hang up and call the number on your card. |
| 4 | Scam (very likely) | Secrecy (a new number), soon hurry | Call your child's old number, or another family member, before replying. |
| 5 | Scam | Fear, hurry | Real companies don't put phone numbers in pop-ups. Close the browser (force quit if needed) and restart. |
| 6 | Scam | Hurry, odd payment (new account) | Call the supplier on the number you already have on file. Never change bank details from an email. |
| 7 | Real | None | A code you asked for, with a warning not to share it. If you didn't ask for it, someone has your password, so change it. |
| 8 | Scam | Hurry, odd payment (crypto) | Hacked friend accounts are common. Ask your friend another way, by calling them. |
| 9 | Can't tell yet | Fear | It could be real or a phishing email. Don't click. Open the app or type the site's address yourself and check your security settings. |
| 10 | Scam | Fear, hurry, odd payment (gift cards) | Courts and police never take gift cards. Hang up and call the court's number from its official website. |
| 11 | Scam | Hurry, secrecy, odd payment (gift cards) | Call or walk over to the CEO and ask, using a number you already had. Report the email. |
| 12 | Real (probably) | None | It asks you to reply or call the office, with no link. If unsure, call the number you already have for the office. |

## Talking points

- The real messages mostly send you somewhere you already know: the app, the back of your card, your usual sign-in. The scams send you somewhere new: their link, their number, their payment method.
- A real message can still look odd. When in doubt, check the same safe way every time.
- Card 7 is a good moment to explain why codes matter: the code is the second key, and the warning in the real message says so.
