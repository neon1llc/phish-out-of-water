## Pace and setup

- Allow 75 minutes, or cover fewer scams. The lab takes longer, and people want to share stories.
- Print everything in at least 14-point type. Use a microphone in a large room.
- Bring a helper for every eight people for the lab.
- Invite caregivers and adult children to come along. Pairs learn faster, and the code word conversation can happen on the spot.

## Which scams to show

Slides 8 (calls and codes), 9 (emergencies), plus the [tech support](../guide/tech-support.md) and [government impostor](../guide/government-impostor.md) stories. Add [romance](../guide/romance-investment.md) if the group is comfortable. It hits widowed and isolated people hard, and naming it without judgment helps.

## What to emphasize

- **Respect.** Many people in the room have spotted more scams than you. Ask for their stories and let them teach each other.
- **The pause needs no technology.** Hanging up and calling back works on any phone.
- **Banks, police, and courts don't collect money at your door.** Scammers sometimes send someone to pick up cash for a "grandchild's bail."
- **The family code word**, set in person, with grandchildren too.
- **Who to call.** Spend extra time on the [who-to-call card](../handouts/who-to-call.md), including the National Elder Fraud Hotline.

## For caregivers

Suggest these, with permission:

- Set up bank alerts for large withdrawals, and consider a trusted contact on the accounts.
- Turn on call screening or silence unknown callers on the phone.
- Place credit freezes with all three bureaus.
- Agree on a "call me first" rule for any request for money, without making it feel like a loss of independence.

[Home Is Where the Smart Is](https://github.com/neon1llc/home-is-where-the-smart-is) has a chapter on helping aging parents with technology.
