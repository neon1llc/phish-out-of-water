## Pace and setup

- 45 to 50 minutes, to fit a class period.
- Use phones as part of the session: students can check their own settings in the lab.
- For high school groups, tell the school counselor about the session in advance and invite them, especially if you cover sextortion.

## Which scams to show

[Job and task scams](../guide/job-scams.md), [marketplace scams](../guide/marketplace.md), [scam texts](../guide/scam-texts.md), and [code theft](../guide/code-theft.md), framed around gaming and social media accounts ("I accidentally sent you my code, can you send it back?").

## What to emphasize

- **Your accounts are worth money.** Gaming, social media, and email accounts get stolen and sold. Two-step sign-in protects them.
- **Never send money or codes to get money.** Any job, prize, or deal where you pay first is a scam.
- **Sextortion.** Scammers pose as someone your age, trade photos, then threaten to share them unless paid. Say clearly: it's not your fault, don't pay, stop replying, keep the messages, and tell a trusted adult. Take It Down (takeitdown.ncmec.org), from the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children, can help remove images of minors. Keep this part calm and factual.
- **Telling someone isn't snitching.** Name the people students can go to.

## Lab twist

Have students check their most-used app's security settings as well as email: two-step sign-in, active sessions, and linked devices.
