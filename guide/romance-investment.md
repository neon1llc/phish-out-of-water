---
title: Romance and investment scams
description: "A new online friend or partner who, after weeks of kindness, introduces an investment, a crypto platform, or an emergency that needs money."
---

## What it looks like

It often starts with a "wrong number" text, a dating app match, or a friendly message on social media. Weeks or months of warm conversation follow. Then the turn: they share a crypto trading platform where they've made a lot, help you invest a little (which seems to grow), and encourage more. Or they have an emergency, a medical bill, a customs fee, a ticket to finally visit.

When you try to withdraw from the "platform," there are fees, taxes, or a freeze. The money is gone.

## The tell

- Someone you've **never met in person**, and who always has a reason not to video call, starts talking about money.
- An investment with guaranteed or unusually high returns.
- Pressure to keep it from family and friends, "who wouldn't understand."

## What to do

Talk to someone you trust before sending any money. Search the platform's name with the word "scam." Never move money to a platform or wallet someone online pointed you to.

## If it already happened

Stop sending money, even for "fees to release your funds." That's a second stage of the same scam. Call your bank. Report it at **ic3.gov** and **ReportFraud.ftc.gov**. Be wary of anyone who later offers to recover the money for a fee; that's a known follow-up scam. These scams cause deep hurt. Talking about it with someone you trust helps.
