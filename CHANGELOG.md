# Changelog

Phishing Awareness Kit releases use `v0.MINOR.PATCH` until the first complete release, v1.0.0, following neon1's [versioning standard](https://neon1.tech/knowledge/standards/versioning/). Each version below has an annotated tag on the commit it names; the release workflow adds missing tags and never moves one.

## v0.1.3 - 2026-11-13

### Changed
- The project is now called Phishing Awareness Kit, in the README, LICENSE, NOTICE, TRADEMARK.md, attributions, slides, and handouts. The credit line in LICENSE, NOTICE, and the handouts uses the new name. Links to the other neon1 education projects use their new names and addresses.
- The workplace notes say a session can be part of yearly awareness training, and that whoever sets a training requirement decides whether it meets it.
- The README says the scope is stable: the repository gets accuracy and source updates, and no new modules for now.

## v0.1.2 - 2026-11-12

### Fixed
- CISA retired Secure Our World and archived its pages. Sources now link CISA's Cybersecurity Awareness Month page, which carries the same four core steps, and the USPIS smishing link points at the article's current address.
- LICENSE names the same code folders as NOTICE and LICENSE-CODE: tools/ and .github/.

## v0.1.1 - 2026-11-12

### Changed
- Every session page, exercise, handout, and field guide entry ends with a Sources section linking the FTC, FBI, USPIS, Justice Department, AARP, NCMEC, and CISA pages behind it.
- Plainer wording throughout, with fewer absolutes and no slogan lines.

### Fixed
- The National Elder Fraud Hotline and AARP helpline entries list their hours, and no longer claim an age limit or membership terms their pages don't state.
- Gift card steps include asking the card company for your money back, as the FTC advises.
- Sextortion advice follows NCMEC: get help before deciding anything about paying, block the account, and keep the messages.
- The USPS card says USPS texts only people who asked for tracking, and those texts contain no links.

## v0.1.0 - 2026-11-12

### Added
- A one-hour session: run sheet, a Marp slide deck, and a facilitator script.
- Four exercises: spot the scam, role-plays, the two-step sign-in lab, and the family code word.
- Handouts: the one-page takeaway, a who-to-call card, a before-and-after quiz, and a feedback form.
- A field guide to eleven common scams, and notes for adapting the hour to families and seniors, workplace teams, and students.
