title: Facilitator script
description: "What to say at each slide, the questions to ask, and how to handle the moments that come up in a scam awareness session."
---

Slide numbers match [slides.md](slides.md). The words in quotes are a guide, not a script to read aloud. Say them your way.

**Tone throughout:** respectful and plain. Many people in the room have spotted more scams than you have. Ask for their stories and thank them for sharing.

## Welcome (slides 1 to 3, 5 minutes)

**Slide 2.** Ask for the show of hands, then the follow-up. Most hands stay up. "You're not alone, and you're not a target because you did something wrong. These messages go to everyone."

Invite one or two short stories. If someone shares that they lost money, thank them and say how common it is. Then move on, so it doesn't become the focus.

**Slide 3.** "By the end of the hour, everyone here will have two-step sign-in turned on for their email. Make sure your phone is nearby."

## The four pressures (slides 4 to 6, 7 minutes)

**Slide 4.** "Scams are built to catch people at a busy or worried moment. The people behind them do this all day, often in organized groups, with scripts."

**Slide 5.** Go through each pressure and ask the room for an example they've heard. Write them on a board if you have one.

**Slide 6.** This is the idea to repeat all session. "If you notice one of these, slow down. If you notice two, stop and check, every time."

## The scams people see (slides 7 to 11, 10 minutes)

Pick the stories that fit your group. The [audiences](../audiences/index.md) page suggests which. For each one, ask "What's the tell?" before you reveal it.

**Slide 7, texts.** "Delivery companies and toll agencies don't text you a link to pay a fine. If you're expecting something, open the company's app or type its address yourself."

**Slide 8, calls.** "This one is growing fast. The caller says they're protecting you, and asks you to read back a code. That code is how they sign in as you. A real fraud team never needs it."

**Slide 9, emergencies.** "If you get a call like this, hang up and call the person on the number you already have, or call another family member. A family code word helps, and we'll set one up at the end."

**Slide 10, invoices.** For workplace groups. "Any change to bank details gets a phone call to a number you already had on file. No exceptions, even for the boss."

**Slide 11, the long game.** "These can go on for months and feel completely real. Eventually the conversation turns to money: an investment, an emergency, a fee to release funds. If someone you've never met in person asks about money, talk to someone you trust first."

## Spot the scam (slide 12, 10 minutes)

Hand out the [card sets](../exercises/spot-the-scam.md). Walk between tables. When time's up, go through four or five cards with the whole room, asking a different table each time. Praise the reasoning more than the right answers. "Can't tell yet" is a great answer when it comes with a way to check.

## The pause (slides 13 to 14, 8 minutes)

**Slide 13.** "Hang up, look up, call back. If the call was real, the company will still be there when you call back."

**Slide 14.** Run one [role-play](../exercises/role-plays.md) in front of the room. You play the scammer, and a volunteer plays themselves. Push a little: "If you hang up, the charge goes through." Cheer when they hang up anyway.

## Lock the front door (slides 15 to 16, 5 minutes)

**Slide 15.** "If someone gets into your email, they can reset almost every other password you have. So email comes first."

Mention password managers briefly: "A password manager remembers a different strong password for every site. The one built into your phone or browser is a fine start."

**Slide 16.** "A sign-in code is like a key someone mails you. If anyone asks you to read it to them, they're trying to use your key."

## Two-step sign-in lab (slide 17, 10 minutes)

Follow the [lab guide](../exercises/two-step-lab.md). Walk the room. Pair people who finish quickly with people who are stuck. If someone can't do it today (forgotten password, no phone), write down their name and offer a follow-up, rather than leaving them feeling behind.

Count hands at the end and say the number out loud.

## Who to tell, and the takeaway (slides 18 to 20, 5 minutes)

**Slide 18.** Fill in the workplace reporting line before the session. For families, suggest choosing a code word tonight, in person, never by text. Hand out the [who-to-call cards](../handouts/who-to-call.md).

**Slide 19.** Hand out the [one-page takeaway](../handouts/one-page-takeaway.md). "Put it on the fridge, or by the phone."

**Slide 20.** Take questions. Collect the [feedback form](../handouts/feedback-form.md) if you're using it.

## Moments that come up

- **"I already lost money."** Thank them. Tell them to call their bank today and report it. The [who-to-call card](../handouts/who-to-call.md) has the steps. Offer to talk after the session.
- **"I'm not good with technology."** "The pause doesn't need any technology. It works on any phone."
- **"Isn't two-step sign-in a hassle?"** "Most services only ask on a new device. It's a few seconds, for a lot of protection."
- **A question you can't answer.** "Good question. I'll find out and send it with the takeaway." Then do.
